How we count reading hours saved

Last updated October 7, 2026

Every number should show its working. This is ours: one formula, one assumption, and a plain list of where the figure is an estimate.

1. The formula

Reading hours saved = items we read × 3 minutes ÷ 60.

2. What counts as an item

An item is one article, post or page that our system reads from the sources we follow. Each item is counted once, from the day we started counting. We count what we read from our own records, not what anyone clicked.

3. Why 3 minutes

Three minutes is about the time to read a typical news article. It is an assumption, not a measurement of any one person: long reads take longer and headlines take less. We chose a modest figure on purpose and we show it next to the number everywhere it appears.

4. What the number is not

It does not claim that you would have read everything we read. It measures the reading we did so that you do not have to. It is not a measure of your personal time, and it is not a promise about what you will save.

5. How the live number moves

The figure is refreshed from our records about every hour. Between refreshes the counter moves at our average pace over the previous 24 hours, never more than two hours past the last refresh, and never backwards. If a refresh is late, the counter stops moving after two hours instead of guessing. If your device asks for reduced motion, it shows one fixed number per visit and does not tick. The tooltip on the number shows when it was last refreshed.

6. The raw figures

The figures behind the counter are public: counters.json.
