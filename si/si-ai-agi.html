SI, AI, AGI and ASI: a plain-English field guide

Four abbreviations, two different meanings of “superintelligence”, and a lot of confusion. This guide defines each term, shows how they relate, and says plainly where experts still disagree.

AI: the umbrella

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the oldest and broadest of the four terms. The phrase appears in the title of a 1955 proposal for a summer 1956 study at Dartmouth College.[3] In US law it is defined as a machine-based system that can, for a given set of human-defined objectives, make predictions, recommendations or decisions influencing real or virtual environments.[2]

That definition is deliberately wide. It covers a system that filters spam, one that recommends a film, and one that writes an essay. “AI” names a family of technologies, not a single product and not a level of ability.

SI: two meanings

“Superintelligence” (SI) now has two meanings, and most confusion comes from mixing them up.

  • The federal meaning. Under Executive Order 14434, signed September 29, 2026, executive-branch agencies are directed to use “Super Intelligence” and “SI” in place of “Artificial Intelligence” and “AI”. For the purposes of the order, SI means the technologies and systems the AI statute already covers.[1][2] It is a naming rule with no capability bar. Document 1 covers it in full.
  • The research meaning. In research and public debate, superintelligence means an intellect much smarter than the best human brains in practically every field. The definition leaves open how it would be built and whether it would be conscious.[4]

When you meet the word, ask which one is meant. The order does not claim that any system has reached the research meaning.

AGI: the middle rung

Artificial general intelligence (AGI) is the idea of a system that can do most of what a person can do, not just one thing. One widely cited paper describes it as an AI system that is at least as capable as a human at most tasks, and calls it “an important and sometimes controversial concept in computing research.”[5]

Because “at least as capable as a human” leaves a lot open, the same authors propose grading systems on two things: how well they perform, and how broad their abilities are. Their performance levels, measured against skilled adults, are:[5]

LevelNamePerformance bar
1EmergingEqual to or somewhat better than an unskilled human
2CompetentAt least the 50th percentile of skilled adults
3ExpertAt least the 90th percentile of skilled adults
4ExceptionalAt least the 99th percentile of skilled adults
5SuperhumanOutperforms 100% of humans

In the authors’ table, as of the September 2025 revision, some general-purpose chatbots sit at Level 1 (Emerging AGI), while general systems at Level 2 and above are listed as “not yet achieved”. That is their assessment, not a consensus.[5]

ASI: the top rung

Artificial superintelligence (ASI) is the research meaning of superintelligence: a system far beyond the best humans, across the board. One early and frequently cited definition describes an intellect that is much smarter than the best human brains in practically every field.[4] In the levels framework above, ASI is the combination of the top performance level (Level 5, outperforms 100% of humans) with generality. The authors list it as “not yet achieved”.[5]

Worth knowing

Superhuman is not the same as general. The same paper classes some single-task systems, such as a chess engine and a protein-structure predictor, as superhuman but narrow: they beat every human at one job and do nothing else.[5] ASI would need to be both.

How they fit together

What is still debated

  • What intelligence is. Two researchers who set out to define machine intelligence begin from the admission that nobody really knows what intelligence is, then propose a formal measure.[6] Other definitions exist.
  • Where “general” begins. The levels above are one proposal. Others draw the line elsewhere, and the paper itself calls the concept controversial.[5]
  • Whether, when and how anything like ASI could exist. There is no consensus. We make no forecast on this page; our outlook lays out scenarios and what to watch.
  • Whether a new name helps. The order says the name “Super Intelligence” more appropriately captures the promise and capabilities of these technologies.[1] Others may find a second meaning of the word confusing. We report both positions and leave the judgement to you.

A 60-second test for any claim

  1. Which term is it? AI, SI, AGI or ASI? They are not interchangeable.
  2. Which meaning of SI? The federal naming rule, or the research idea of an intellect beyond the best humans?
  3. What kind of claim is it? A definition, a measurement, a forecast, or a product claim?
  4. Can you check it? A good source links to the primary text. Treat an unlinked claim as a rumor until you can.

Glossary

Narrow AI
A system built for one kind of task. Can be extremely good at it.
General AI, AGI
A system with broad ability across many kinds of tasks, at least as capable as a human at most of them.[5]
Superhuman
Outperforming every human at a task. Narrow systems can already be superhuman at a single task.[5]
Artificial superintelligence, ASI
A general system far beyond the best humans in practically every field.[4]
Statutory definition
The definition of “artificial intelligence” in US law, 15 U.S.C. 9401(3), to which the executive order points.[1][2]
